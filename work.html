01 · Mobile Game

BRICK FURY

A space-themed brick breaker built in Flutter and Flame. I did all of it: game design, the interface system, HUD, economy, naming, store assets and every line of code. The goal was an arcade game that feels loud and physical, with an interface around it that stays calm and readable.

RoleSolo: design, art direction, code
PlatformAndroid, production ready
StackFlutter, Flame, Firebase
ScopeCampaign, endless, daily, Forge, Lab
Decision 01 · HUD

A quiet metal frame around a loud playfield

The HUD is a metallic sci-fi frame with neon side rails coloured by difficulty, and fixed docks for power-up timers. Score, wave and currency sit in one strip at the top, so nothing competes with the ball.

Why: the bricks already use five saturated colours. A HUD just as loud would make players lose the ball, so colour on the frame carries one meaning only: difficulty.

Decision 02 · Damage

Cracks instead of colour shifts

Early builds showed brick health by changing colour. I replaced that with a crack-and-chunk system: bricks visibly fracture and shed pieces as they take hits.

Why: colour already meant brick type, so it couldn't also mean health. Cracks read instantly, don't rely on colour vision, and make every hit feel physical.

Decision 03 · Feedback

Callouts with a clear pecking order

Hit words (SNAP, CRISP, BLAZING, GODLIKE) grow in size and warmth as the combo builds. Point values stay small and white, and rare moments like DOUBLE STRIKE and FURY MODE get the biggest type.

Why: the player should feel the reward without reading. The type hierarchy does the explaining.

Decision 04 · Naming

LUXIUM, not "Laxium"

The premium currency and its fragments are LUXIUM and LUXIUM SHARD. "Laxium" was dropped early because of the laxative association, and "Brick Fury" went through trademark checks before any art was made.

Why: a name is part of the interface. It has to sound right when someone says it out loud.

02 · Sports Product

3SCORERS

A fantasy football and prediction app with live match data. I lead the mobile team: I own the Flutter architecture and build the animated parts of the product (player cards, match moments, the isometric city) with CustomPainter, Rive and Flame.

RoleMobile Team Lead
SinceJune 2023
StackFlutter, Bloc, Rive, Flame
Live Google Play · 3scorers.com

Match detail and player profile. Club colour tints the header so you know which team you're looking at before reading a word; the stats sit on a neutral surface so they stay legible.

Motion

Animation only where it means something

Player card reveals, goal moments and reward drops are animated in Rive and CustomPainter. Lists, tables and settings stay still.

Why: when everything moves, nothing stands out. Motion is kept for moments that change how the user feels.

World

An isometric city as the home screen

The club world is a 3D isometric city canvas with assets delivered over the air, so new buildings and events ship without an app update.

Why: most fantasy apps look like spreadsheets. A place you can visit gives people a reason to come back between matchdays.

Engineering

Architecture a team can move fast in

Clean Architecture with Bloc, widget and unit tests on every controller, and code review for every change. I mentor the junior developers on the team.

Why: live sports data changes every minute. The codebase has to make the next feature cheap, not scary.

Brand

A coin that feels worth earning

The in-app currency is a gold, minted version of the 3Scorers mark, so rewards look and feel like the brand itself.

04 · Approach

HOW I JUDGE A SCREEN

The checklist I use on my own work and in code and design reviews. Each step has to pass before the next one is worth discussing.

  1. 01

    Does it answer the first question?

    In three seconds, can you tell what the screen is for and what to do next? If not, nothing else matters yet.

  2. 02

    Is there one clear hierarchy?

    One primary action, one focal point. Size, weight and contrast should agree with each other.

  3. 03

    Does every colour carry one meaning?

    If a colour means "danger" in one place and "brand" in another, the user pays for it. Brick Fury's crack system came from this rule.

  4. 04

    Is the spacing a system?

    Consistent rhythm and aligned edges, with no values picked by eye. Uneven spacing is the fastest giveaway of rushed work.

  5. 05

    Are all the states designed?

    Empty, loading, error, partial, full. The happy path is only a fraction of the real screen.

  6. 06

    Does the motion earn its place?

    Every animation should explain a change or reward an action. Anything else is decoration.

  7. 07

    Is it on-brand, or just on-trend?

    Good work looks like it could only belong to this product, not like every other app released this year.
